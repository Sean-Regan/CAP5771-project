Our data doesn't lend itself strongly to any measure of categorization/prediction, rather we are focused more on clustering to find and visualize distinct patterns
in the data itself.

Originally, we used OPTICS for clustering, but given the dimensionality of our data, KMeans was more than enough for visualizing simple patterns.
Since we are pulling in zip codes we assume that locality will matter somewhat for the outputs of our clustering, as well as nutrient types.

To find our clusters, we fit a regression model (to predict price based on features (nutriend id, lat/lon of the price, store, and brand)) and then 
clustering the residual differences between the predicted prices and actual prices.
This allows us to determine the if there are disparities from the local norm for each nutrient (since prices 
will always change between areas, but should follow patterns that would be modeled in the regression), and allows us to cluster 
based on the magnitude of these differences.

Since we are clustering, and not predicting or categorizing, we don't have a need for train/validation/test splitting.
